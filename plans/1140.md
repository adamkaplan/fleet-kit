### Cause (clause 1, stated first)
`triage_refresh` in `bin/fleet-switchboard` builds `todo` from every (repo, unit) that is missing or older than `TRIAGE_TTL` (900 s), as `(held["at"] or 0, repo, unit)`, and reads `sorted(todo)`: the stalest first, and with a cold start (`state.units` empty, every `at` equal to 0) ties broken by repository name. It stops when `budget[0] - reserve < 1` or after `TRIAGE_PASS_SECONDS` (10 s); the budget is a share of `TRIAGE_CALLS_PER_PASS` (30) less the reserve for fresh checks, and the triage runs about every 15 minutes. Six repositories with issues, comments, merged and one list per awaiting label are about 30 cold units, so they are read a few per pass and take hours. `alert_wake_pass` needs `issues:<repo>` whole: `whole_skipped` finds `read_whole` unmarked (`triage_mark_unit` says "issues not read yet" while `state.units[(repo, "issues")]` is missing) and writes one `whole.skipped` line saying only "issues not read yet". Nothing in the order puts the alert repositories' `issues` units first. That is the recorded case: 13 skip lines from 11:41Z to 13:18Z and 81 units still unchecked for "call budget" at 14:01Z.

### Approach (what, not how)
The refresh orders the not-yet-read `issues` units of the repositories in `alert_labels` before every other unit not yet read, then the old order. This costs the other units at most one list per alert repository. The skip line says how far the cold start has got: units read of units wanted. The docs state the order.

### Done when (copied exactly from the ask)
Done when: (1) the cause is found and stated first, from the recorded case (the audit held 13 `whole.skipped` lines for the alert wake, input `issues:<repository>`, reason "issues not read yet", from 11:41Z to 13:18Z, and the triage summary at 14:01Z still listed 81 units unchecked for "call budget"): after a start the triage's in-memory units are empty, the refresh reads the stalest units first with equal ties broken by repository name, the pass spends at most `pass_seconds` (10) and a share of `calls_per_pass` (30), and the triage runs at most about every 15 minutes, so about 30 cold units (six repositories, each with issues, comments, merged and one list per awaiting label) take hours to read once, and the alert wake needs one of them whole; (2) the refresh reads the `issues` unit of every repository in `alert_labels` before any other unit that is not yet read, and it never starves the other units for more than the one list per such repository; (3) a test: a config with six repositories, one named in `alert_labels`, and a call budget that reads four units per pass: after the first triage pass the alert repository's `issues` input is whole and the wake can send; with a mutant that keeps the old order failing it; (4) the skip line says how far the cold start has got (units read of units wanted) so a waiting lane sees progress, not only "not read yet"; (5) the documentation says the order.
