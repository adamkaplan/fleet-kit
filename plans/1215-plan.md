## Cause (found first, from the recorded case)

Recorded case: the owner declined to approve a change only because it touched a path on the matrix, with no reason why it was his. The delegated clear's ceiling follows the rating label and the path class, not what the change can do. Reading main:

- `bin/impact_floor.py`, `owner_decide(matrix, paths, impacts, ...)`: builds every hold reason as a bare string (`"top tier path (%s): %s"`, the `clear_code_paths` reason, `"rating: ... class %s"`) and returns only "hold", "second-review" or "none". Inputs are the changed paths and the rating's class: `top_tier_hits`, `owner_hold_rule`, `clear_code_paths`, `narrow_paths` read `docs/review-laws/impact-matrix.json` (`owner_hold`: top tier classes credentials, identity, authority; `top_tier_paths`; `narrow_by_diff`). The reason says which path matched, never what the change can do or why it is the owner's.
- `bin/fleet-switchboard`, `pr_floor_report` and `cmd_impact_floor` (`impact floor`): print `owner_decision` and `owner_reasons` (those strings) and `top_tier_hits`. No brief exists. `cmd_impact_matrix` only prints.
- `bin/impact_hold.py`, `_apply_impact_hold_locked` (the daemon's hold line): applies the labels and the audit line from that same report, so the hold line repeats the path rule.
- `bin/delegated_clear.py`, `who_can_clear`, `refusal_items`, `judge` (the score by rating level, `rating_roles`, `runs_required`) and the matrix block `delegated_clear` (`never_path_classes`, `top_tier_review_classes`, `clear_code_paths`, `closure_files`, `new_file_globs`): the delegated clear's ceiling is the rating label's level and the path class. A top tier class or a machinery path is refused for the Chief of Staff whatever the change does: this is the cause of the recorded case.
- `bin/impact_hold.py`, `impact_clear_core` (head-bound: refused when the head moved; audit event `gate.hold_cleared` with `by`, `reason`, `head`, `under_order`) records the clear; the audit line carries a free-text reason only, no brief. A clear with a stale or absent brief cannot be refused today because no brief exists.
- `bin/fleet-switchboard`, `_held_defects` (gate G6b, `pr-explain --check`, registered in `bin/plan_ledger.py`): checks form only (`## Why this needs you`, Before/After, Example, Questions), no one-sentence rationale rule; the Decisions list (`derive_decisions`, `decisions_from_*`) shows the path rule.

Re-verified against main: no brief, no brief-bound clear, no substance-based ceiling, no rationale rule exist. The backstops of clause 5 already exist and must not change: the author-clear refusal and independent roles (`judge`, `rating_roles`, ledger `authors_of`), the two independent runs (`RUNS_REQUIRED`), the head binding (`impact_clear_core` head check, clear state per head), the void on a push (clear keyed by head and fingerprint).

## Constraints

- PC-1: The pull request that implements this is held for the owner, ONE pull request, with the owner's order of 22 and 23 October quoted as the rationale at the top of its body. Reason: it changes who may clear.
- PC-2: Mechanical backstops are unchanged in behaviour: no self-clear, no author-clear, two independent reads at the head for a machinery path, head-bound clear, a push voids. The brief only changes WHAT the Chief of Staff may clear on the substance; it never lifts a backstop. Reason: the owner's order relaxes judgement, not the mechanics.
- PC-3: A brief is built from the diff and the records (ledger, CI, ratings, blast radius reads), never from the PR text alone; a piece that cannot be read is printed "not shown" and counts as unknown, never as safe. A brief with any "not shown" piece in the risk lines recommends the owner. Reason: fail closed.
- PC-4: The brief fits one screen (at most 24 lines of at most 100 characters) and is deterministic: the same diff and records give byte-identical output, so the clear can compare it. The audit line stores the brief and its hash.
- PC-5: Escalation is by the brief's material risks only (credentials or issuance widened, spend, irreversible, speaks for the owner), read from the diff by the floor; a class name, path or rating label alone neither escalates nor clears.
- PC-6: Any held-pull-request label rule (hold-impact) stays owner-tier: an advisory pull request (no material loosening) carries no owner-hold label; a person's own hold label stays lifted by the owner only (the existing refusal in `impact_clear_core`).
- PC-7: Public-safe names in docs, examples and fixtures.

## Out of scope

- A model-written brief: the brief is deterministic from the diff and records.
- Changing the rating levels, the plan gates, or the merge shim; the clear of a pull request by anyone but the Chief of Staff or owner.
- Lifting backstops (clause 5) or the owner's own plain clear.

## Areas touched

- `bin/impact_floor.py` (brief builder; held machinery: the floor), `bin/delegated_clear.py` and `bin/impact_hold.py` (ceiling by substance, brief-bound clear, audit line; held machinery: `clear_code_paths`), `docs/review-laws/impact-matrix.json` (held machinery: the matrix), `bin/fleet-switchboard` (`cmd_impact_floor`, `_held_defects`, Decisions; held machinery), tests, `docs/switchboard.md`. Yes: all but docs and tests are held machinery, so ONE pull request held for the owner (PC-1).

## Risks and rollback

- Risk: a brief that under-reports a risk lets the Chief of Staff clear something that matters. Mitigation: fail closed on "not shown", risks read from the diff (PC-3, PC-5), backstops unchanged (PC-2), every clear audited with its brief.
- Risk: the brief's text drifts between the floor and the clear: mitigated by the hash compare (S12).
- Rollback: revert the one pull request; the old class ceiling returns; recorded audit lines stay readable.

## Open questions

1. Is a material-risk list closed at the four named (credentials or issuance widened, spend, irreversible, speaks for the owner)? The plan assumes yes; a fifth needs the owner.
2. How is "mutants run" and "blast radius" read from the records when none are recorded: printed "not shown" (assumed), recommending the owner.
3. Whether the recommended decider is advice only (assumed: the clear compares briefs, it does not obey the recommendation).
