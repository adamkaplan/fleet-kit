### Cause (clause 1, stated first)
A reminder is keyed in `add_reminder` (`bin/switchboard/reminders.py`, about line 77): `--key` is stored as `item["key"]` and the sender as `item["from"]`. When armed again, line 95 removes the pending items with the same key and the same sender whose `at` is later than `created`: "one pending per owner and key; another owner or key, a fired one and a sender-less one are never touched". A fired (due) copy is handled by `ReminderFacts.superseded` (about line 408) and `ReminderFacts.__call__` (about line 419): a due item with a newer due-or-fired item of the same owner and key is dropped by `drop_superseded_item` (about line 281, under the lock, only if the kept one is still in the file) and audited as `reminder.drop_superseded` with `dropped` and `kept`.

What #1285 already covers: (a) a re-arm replaces the pending (future) copy; (b) a fired copy still in `reminders.json` is dropped when a newer copy of the same owner and key is due or fired, at the moment the facts are built; another owner or key is never matched.

What is NEW (the two open cases):
1. Cancel of a chain. `remind_manage` (about line 603, the `--cancel` branch about line 626) cancels ONE reminder by id (`remove_reminders`, audit `reminder.cancel`). There is no cancel by owner and key, so a chain armed with `--key` cannot be stopped as a unit, and a fired copy of it can still be delivered.
2. The in-flight copy. `ReminderFacts.__call__` makes a `reminder:<id>` fact and only afterwards does the engine deliver it; the item stays in the file until `prune_delivered_reminders` sees its key in the transcript. The supersede check and the lock in `drop_superseded_item` run when the facts are built, not at the delivery step. A copy that fired, became a fact, and is then superseded by a re-arm or cancelled before it is delivered is still delivered. The #1285 live check shows only the fired-in-file case; the race is not shown to hold, so clause (3) stays in scope.

### Approach (what, not how)
Cancel by owner and key drops every waiting copy of that chain (pending and fired, same sender and key only) in one locked step and writes ONE audit line `cancelled` naming the chain; a copy already delivered is not recalled. At the delivery step each reminder fact is checked once more against the file: if its item is gone (cancelled) or a newer copy of the same owner and key exists (superseded), it is not delivered and the drop is audited naming both reminders (the dropped and the replacing one, or "cancelled"). Another owner's or another key's reminder is never matched. Tests use a scratch state directory only.
